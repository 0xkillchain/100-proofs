
# Proof 73: The New Testament Was Written in Greek

## The Claim

The New Testament was written in Greek — not Hebrew or Aramaic — because its primary audience was Greek-speaking Europeans. The scattered Israelites in Europe spoke Greek, so the apostles wrote in their language.

---

## The Fact

### The Language

All 27 New Testament books were written in Greek:
- Koine (common) Greek
- The lingua franca of the eastern Mediterranean
- The language of the educated across the Roman Empire

No original NT book survives in Hebrew or Aramaic.

---

## Why Greek?

### Standard Explanation

Greek was the common language of:
- Alexander's former empire
- The eastern Roman Empire
- Trade and commerce
- International communication

### The Deeper Question

But why did God choose Greek for His final revelation?

Options:
1. Mere convenience (the "practical" view)
2. Greek was Israel's language in diaspora
3. The audience was Greek-speaking Israelites

---

## The Septuagint Connection

### Israel Already Used Greek

By the 3rd century BC:
- Jews in Alexandria needed Greek Scripture
- The Septuagint was produced (~250 BC)
- Diaspora Israelites spoke Greek, not Hebrew

When the NT was written:
- The Septuagint was Israel's Bible
- Greek was Israel's language
- Writing in Greek reached Israelites

---

## The Audience

### To Whom Was the NT Written?

| Book | Audience | Language Implication |
|------|----------|---------------------|
| Romans | Rome | Latin possible, but Greek used |
| Corinthians | Corinth, Greece | Greek obvious |
| Galatians | Galatia (Anatolia) | Greek-speaking region |
| Ephesians | Ephesus (Anatolia) | Greek city |
| Thessalonians | Thessalonica (Greece) | Greek city |
| Hebrews | Scattered "Hebrews" | Greek used |

The audience was Greek-speaking — the scattered Israelites.

---

## Greek Philosophy and Theology

### Logos

> **John 1:1** — "In the beginning was the **Logos**, and the Logos was with God, and the Logos was God."

John used the Greek concept of "Logos" (Word/Reason) to describe Christ. This communicated to Greek-speaking, philosophically-trained Israelites.

### Translation of Concepts

Greek provided vocabulary for:
- *Ekklesia* (assembly/church)
- *Hagioi* (holy ones/saints)
- *Christos* (anointed/Messiah)
- *Soter* (savior)

Hebrew concepts found Greek expression.

---

## Not Hebrew

### Why Not Hebrew?

If the NT was primarily for Jews in Judea:
- Hebrew would make more sense
- Aramaic was spoken locally
- Hebrew was the sacred language

But the NT is in Greek because:
- The audience was scattered Israel
- Scattered Israel spoke Greek
- Judea was not the primary mission field

### The Exception Proves the Rule

The Gospel of Matthew may have had a Hebrew original (according to tradition). But:
- Only Greek Matthew survives
- It was translated for the wider audience
- Even Judean content reached Greeks

---

## Preservation

### Who Preserved the Greek NT?

The Greek New Testament was preserved by:
- Greek-speaking churches
- European Christians
- Copied throughout Christendom
- Transmitted through European institutions

Europeans (Israel) preserved their own Scriptures.

---

## The Significance

### God's Providence

God chose Greek because:
- His people spoke Greek
- The Scriptures would reach them
- Greek philosophical vocabulary communicated truth
- It was Israel's language in diaspora

### Not for Jews

If the NT was primarily for Jews:
- Hebrew would be expected
- Temple Judaism would be the context
- But neither is the case

The NT in Greek points to scattered Israel as the audience.

---

## Synthesis

The NT in Greek proves:

1. **Audience was Greek-speaking** — Scattered Israelites
2. **Septuagint connection** — Already using Greek OT
3. **European destination** — Greece, Rome, Anatolia
4. **Logos vocabulary** — Communicated to Greeks
5. **European preservation** — Israel kept their Scriptures

The New Testament was written in Greek for Greek-speaking Israelites — the Europeans.
