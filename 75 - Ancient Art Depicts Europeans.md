
# Proof 75: Ancient Art Depicts Europeans

## The Claim

Ancient Near Eastern art consistently depicts Israelites and related peoples with European features, light skin, varied hair colors, and Caucasian characteristics. This visual evidence predates modern racial theories.

---

## Egyptian Art

### Racial Distinctions

Egyptian tomb paintings distinguished peoples:
- **Egyptians**: Brown/copper skin
- **Nubians**: Black skin, African features
- **Libyans**: Light skin, blonde/red hair
- **Asiatics**: Light to medium skin, beards

Egyptians were meticulous about depicting different peoples accurately.

### Israelites in Egypt

Paintings showing Semitic peoples (including Israelites):
- Lighter than Egyptians
- Multi-colored robes
- Beards
- Distinct from Nubians

---

## Beni Hasan Painting

### ~1900 BC

A tomb painting at Beni Hasan shows Asiatic traders:
- Light skin (painted lighter than Egyptians)
- Colorful clothing
- Beards
- Clear ethnic distinction

This is from the patriarchal era (~Abraham).

---

## Assyrian Reliefs

### Israelites Depicted

The Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III (~841 BC):
- Shows King Jehu of Israel paying tribute
- Israelites depicted with beards
- Semitic appearance (similar to Assyrians)
- Not black, not distinctively different from Assyrians

### Lachish Reliefs (~701 BC)

Sennacherib's siege of Lachish:
- Judean defenders shown
- Bearded men
- Similar appearance to Assyrians
- No indication of non-Caucasian features

---

## Persian Art

### Persepolis

The Apadana reliefs at Persepolis show:
- Various tribute-bearing peoples
- Each group distinguished
- Caucasian features for Israelite-region peoples
- African features for African delegations

The Persians distinguished between races visually.

---

## Classical Art

### Greek Vase Paintings

Greek art depicts:
- Greeks as light-skinned
- Various peoples with distinct features
- Ethiopians as black
- Persians/Asians differently from Africans

### Roman Art

Fayum mummy portraits (~1st-3rd century AD):
- Mediterranean people of Roman Egypt
- European features common
- This is the era of Christ and apostles

---

## Early Christian Art

### Catacombs of Rome (~2nd-4th century)

The earliest Christian art shows:
- Christ with European features
- Apostles as Romans/Greeks
- Biblical figures in Roman dress
- No attempt at "Middle Eastern" appearance

### Dura-Europos Synagogue (~3rd century)

Jewish synagogue in Syria:
- Moses depicted with European features
- Biblical scenes in Greco-Roman style
- Light skin, varied appearances

---

## Byzantine Art

### Christ Pantocrator

The iconic Byzantine depiction:
- Long face
- Light brown hair
- Light eyes (often)
- Beard
- European features

This became the standard image, originating in the Eastern Roman Empire.

---

## Medieval Art

### Consistent Depiction

Throughout medieval Europe:
- Christ, Mary, apostles: European
- Prophets and patriarchs: European
- Angels: European
- No distinction between "Semitic" and "European"

Artists depicted what they knew, themselves and their ancestors.

---

## What This Means

### Ancient Artists Had No Agenda

1. **Egyptians** depicted peoples as they saw them
2. **Assyrians** distinguished groups accurately
3. **Early Christians** depicted their own people
4. **No modern racial theory** influenced them

### Modern "Dark Middle Eastern" Image

The depiction of Israelites as dark "Middle Eastern" is:
- A modern invention (19th-20th century)
- Influenced by racial theories
- Contradicted by ancient art
- Possibly deliberate deception

---

## Synthesis

Ancient art proves:

1. **Egyptian distinction**: Israelites different from Nubians
2. **Assyrian depictions**: Semitic, not African
3. **Early Christian art**: European features
4. **Byzantine tradition**: Light-skinned Christ
5. **Medieval continuity**: No distinction made

Ancient peoples depicted Israelites with European features because Israelites looked European.
