
# Proof 14: Ancient Artwork and Depictions

## The Claim

Ancient artwork depicting Israelites, Christ, and the apostles consistently shows European features. This visual evidence was created before modern racial politics and reflects the actual appearance of these peoples.

---

## Egyptian Depictions

### Tomb Paintings

Egyptian tomb paintings distinguished between peoples by skin color and features:

| People | Egyptian Depiction |
|--------|-------------------|
| Egyptians | Brown/copper skin |
| Nubians | Black skin |
| Libyans | Light skin, blonde/red hair |
| Asiatics (including Israelites) | Light to medium skin, varied hair |

### The Beni Hasan Tomb Painting

- Location: Egypt
- Date: ~1900 BC (time of the patriarchs)
- Subject: A Semitic trading party entering Egypt
- Features shown:
  - Light skin (lighter than Egyptians)
  - Multi-colored robes
  - Beards
  - Distinct from Egyptians and Nubians

### Thutmose III's Campaigns

Egyptian records of campaigns in Canaan show the peoples as:
- Distinct from Egyptians
- Lighter-skinned
- Bearded
- Wearing distinctive clothing

---

## Assyrian Depictions

### The Black Obelisk of Shalmaneser III

- Date: 841 BC
- Subject: King Jehu of Israel paying tribute
- Features shown:
  - Bearded men
  - Distinctive Israelite dress
  - Semitic features

### Lachish Reliefs

- Location: Nineveh (now in British Museum)
- Date: 701 BC
- Subject: Sennacherib's siege of Lachish (Judean city)
- Judean defenders shown with:
  - Beards
  - Distinctive dress
  - Features similar to Assyrians (both Semitic)

---

## Greek and Roman Depictions

### Fayum Mummy Portraits

- Location: Roman Egypt
- Date: 1st-3rd century AD
- Subjects: Greco-Roman Egyptians
- Features:
  - Mediterranean appearance
  - Varied — some quite European in appearance
  - Contemporary with the apostolic era

### Roman Artwork

Roman art of the 1st-4th centuries shows:
- Europeans as they actually appeared
- Varied features across the empire
- Christ and apostles depicted as Roman/European in early Christian art

---

## Early Christian Art

### Catacombs of Rome

The earliest Christian art (2nd-4th century) in Roman catacombs depicts:

**Christ:**
- Young, beardless (early) or bearded (later)
- European features
- Roman-style clothing

**The Apostles:**
- Roman appearance
- Togas and Roman dress
- European features

**Biblical Scenes:**
- Jonah, Daniel, Moses — all depicted as Romans/Europeans
- No attempt to make them look "Middle Eastern" in modern sense

### The Good Shepherd

One of the earliest Christian images — Christ as the Good Shepherd:
- Young man
- European features
- Roman pastoral dress
- Carrying a lamb

### Dura-Europos Synagogue

- Location: Syria
- Date: 3rd century AD
- Significance: Oldest surviving synagogue paintings
- Features:
  - Moses depicted with European features
  - Biblical figures in Greco-Roman style
  - No modern "Middle Eastern" appearance

---

## Byzantine Art

### Christ Pantocrator

The iconic Byzantine image of Christ:
- Long face
- Beard
- Light brown hair
- Light eyes (often depicted)
- European features

This tradition began in the 6th century and continued throughout the Byzantine period.

### The Mandylion

The Image of Edessa (Mandylion) — tradition of Christ's face on cloth:
- Bearded
- Long hair
- Solemn expression
- European appearance

### Icons of the Theotokos

Mary depicted in Byzantine icons:
- Dark hair
- European features
- Mediterranean appearance

---

## Medieval European Art

### Illuminated Manuscripts

From the Book of Kells to the Lindisfarne Gospels:
- Christ and apostles depicted as Europeans
- No distinction made between "Semitic" and European
- Artists depicted what they knew — themselves

### Cathedral Art

Romanesque and Gothic cathedrals across Europe:
- Christ, Mary, apostles, prophets — all European in appearance
- Stained glass, sculpture, paintings — consistent European depiction
- Created by Europeans who understood these figures as their ancestors

---

## The Magi

### Traditional Depiction

The three Magi (wise men) were traditionally depicted as:
- Three ages of man (young, middle, old)
- European appearance (until later additions)
- Representing the three known continents

### Later Changes

The tradition of making one Magus African developed in the 12th-15th centuries — **after** the original European-only tradition.

Original European depictions:
- Ravenna mosaics (6th century) — all European
- Early medieval art — all European
- Later added diversity for symbolic reasons

## What This Evidence Shows

### Ancient Artists Had No Agenda

1. **Egyptians** depicted peoples as they saw them — and showed Israelites as distinct from themselves and from Nubians
2. **Early Christians** depicted Christ and the apostles as Europeans because they were Europeans depicting their own people
3. **Byzantine artists** continued this tradition naturally
4. **Medieval Europeans** understood biblical figures as their ancestors

### The Modern Revision

The depiction of Israelites/Christ/apostles as dark "Middle Eastern" people is a **modern** revision:
- Developed in the 19th-20th centuries
- Influenced by racial theories that separated "Semites" from "Aryans"
- Contradicts the artistic record before that era

---

## Synthesis

Ancient artwork demonstrates:

1. **Egyptian records** — Israelites were distinct from Nubians (African)
2. **Assyrian records** — Israelites depicted as Semitic (similar to Assyrians, not African)
3. **Early Christian art** — Christ and apostles depicted as European
4. **Byzantine tradition** — Consistent European depiction
5. **Medieval art** — Europeans depicted biblical figures as themselves

Ancient artists had no reason to falsify. They depicted what they knew. The consistent European appearance of biblical figures in pre-modern art reflects reality: these were the ancestors of the Europeans who painted them.
