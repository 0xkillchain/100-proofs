
# Proof 76: The Magi Were Depicted as Europeans

## The Claim

The Magi (wise men) who visited Christ were traditionally depicted as Europeans in early Christian art. The later addition of an African Magus was symbolic, not historical. The original depictions show them as European.

---

## The Biblical Account

### Matthew 2:1-2

> "Now when Jesus was born in Bethlehem of Judaea in the days of Herod the king, behold, there came **wise men** from the east to Jerusalem, Saying, Where is he that is born King of the Jews? for we have seen his star in the east, and are come to worship him."

The text says:
- Wise men (*magoi*)
- From the east
- Knew of the "King of the Jews"
- Followed a star
- Number not specified

---

## Who Were the Magi?

### Historical Background

"Magi" (*magoi*) were:
- Persian/Median priestly class
- Known for astronomy/astrology
- Connected to Israelite prophecy (through Daniel's influence)
- Possibly Israelite-influenced

### Why They Knew

Daniel had been chief of the wise men in Babylon:
> **Daniel 2:48**: "The king made Daniel a great man... and made him ruler over all the wise men of Babylon."

The Magi may have preserved Israelite prophecies through Daniel's influence.

---

## Early Depictions

### Catacomb Art (~3rd-4th century)

The earliest depictions of the Magi show:
- All depicted as similar (no racial distinction)
- Persian/Phrygian clothing
- European features
- Same age or similar ages

### Ravenna Mosaics (~6th century)

Sant'Apollinare Nuovo in Ravenna:
- Three Magi named: Balthasar, Melchior, Gaspar
- All depicted with European features
- Persian dress
- No African among them

---

## The Later Addition

### When Did the African Magus Appear?

The tradition of one African Magus developed:
- Gradually from 12th century onward
- Fully established by 14th-15th century
- Symbolic, not historical
- Represented Africa/Ham receiving Christ

### The Three Ages

Before racial diversification, the Magi represented:
- Youth (Gaspar)
- Middle age (Balthasar)
- Old age (Melchior)

This symbolized all stages of life worshipping Christ.

### The Three Continents

Later, the Magi represented:
- Europe (white)
- Asia (sometimes depicted as Asian)
- Africa (black)

This was theological symbolism, not historical fact.

---

## Original Tradition

### All European/Persian

The original tradition shows:
- All Magi from the East (Persia/Media)
- All with similar features
- No racial diversity depicted
- Persian clothing consistent

### Why Europeans?

If the Magi came from Parthia/Persia:
- The region had Israelite diaspora
- Scythians (Israelites) were in the area
- The prophecies of Balaam were known there
- Israelite wise men would recognize Israelite Messiah

---

## The Numbers Prophecy

### Numbers 24:17

> "There shall come a **Star out of Jacob**, and a **Sceptre shall rise out of Israel**."

Balaam prophesied this in Moab. The Magi knew:
- A star would mark the King
- He would come from Israel

How did eastern Magi know Israelite prophecy? Because they had Israelite connection.

---

## Modern Depictions

### Diverse Magi Now Standard

Today's nativity scenes typically show:
- One white Magus
- One black Magus
- One Asian Magus

This is modern symbolism, not ancient tradition.

### Recovery of Original

Understanding the original depiction:
- All were European/Persian in appearance
- No racial diversity in earliest art
- The later change was theological, not historical

---

## Synthesis

The Magi depiction proves:

1. **Earliest art**: All European/Persian
2. **Later development**: African Magus added symbolically
3. **Historical probability**: Persian Magi would have Israelite connection
4. **Prophecy knowledge**: Knew Balaam's prediction
5. **Israelite link**: Diaspora influence in the East

The Magi were originally depicted as Europeans because they were connected to Israelites in the eastern diaspora.
