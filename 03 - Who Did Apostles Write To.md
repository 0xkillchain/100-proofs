
# Proof 3: Who Did the Apostles Write To?

## The Claim

The apostles wrote their epistles to people in Europe and Anatolia (modern Turkey), Greek and Roman territories. They wrote to Europeans because Europeans are the scattered Israelites.

---

## Scriptural Evidence

### Peter's Epistles

> **1 Peter 1:1**: "Peter, an apostle of Jesus Christ, to the **strangers scattered** throughout **Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia, and Bithynia**."

All five regions are in Anatolia (modern Turkey), Greek-speaking, European-settled territories.

The Greek word for "strangers scattered" is *parepidemos* (sojourners) combined with *diaspora* (dispersion). This is the same word used for the dispersion of Israel.

> **1 Peter 2:9-10**: "But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an holy nation, a peculiar people... Which in time past were not a people, but are now the people of God: which had not obtained mercy, but now have obtained mercy."

This directly quotes Hosea 1:9-10, which was written specifically about the Northern Kingdom of Israel being divorced and scattered, then later restored.

### James' Epistle

> **James 1:1**: "James, a servant of God and of the Lord Jesus Christ, to the **twelve tribes which are scattered abroad**, greeting."

James explicitly states he is writing to the twelve tribes in dispersion. He was not writing to Jews in Judea, he was writing to scattered Israelites, who were in Europe.

### Paul's Epistles All European Destinations

| Epistle | Location | Region |
|---------|----------|--------|
| Romans | Rome | Italy |
| 1 & 2 Corinthians | Corinth | Greece |
| Galatians | Galatia | Anatolia |
| Ephesians | Ephesus | Anatolia |
| Philippians | Philippi | Macedonia (Greece) |
| Colossians | Colossae | Anatolia |
| 1 & 2 Thessalonians | Thessalonica | Macedonia (Greece) |

Paul also wrote to individuals:
- Timothy (in Ephesus)
- Titus (in Crete)
- Philemon (in Colossae)

**Every destination was European or in European-settled Anatolia.**

### Paul Confirms He Writes to Israel

> **Romans 9:3-4**: "For I could wish that myself were accursed from Christ for my brethren, my kinsmen according to the flesh: Who are **Israelites**; to whom pertaineth the adoption, and the glory, and the covenants, and the giving of the law, and the service of God, and the promises."

Paul calls the Romans his "kinsmen" and "Israelites."

> **Romans 11:1**: "I say then, Hath God cast away his people? God forbid. For I also am an **Israelite**, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin."

Paul identifies himself as an Israelite writing to fellow Israelites.

### John's Letters

> **Revelation 1:4**: "John to the **seven churches which are in Asia**."

The seven churches (Ephesus, Smyrna, Pergamos, Thyatira, Sardis, Philadelphia, Laodicea) were all in Anatolia, Greek cities.

---

## The Pattern

### Where the Apostles Went

| Apostle | Primary Mission Field |
|---------|----------------------|
| Peter | Anatolia, possibly Britain |
| Paul | Greece, Rome, Anatolia, Spain |
| James | Jerusalem (to scattered tribes visiting) |
| John | Ephesus (Anatolia) |
| Andrew | Scythia (according to tradition) |
| Thomas | Parthia (where deported Israelites lived) |

The apostles went where the Israelites had scattered, **not** to Africa, **not** to the Far East, **not** to the Americas.

### Christ's Command

> **Matthew 10:5-6**: "These twelve Jesus sent forth, and commanded them, saying, Go not into the way of the Gentiles, and into any city of the Samaritans enter ye not: But go rather to the **lost sheep of the house of Israel**."

The apostles obeyed. They went to Europe. Therefore, Europe contained the lost sheep of Israel.

---

## The Term "Gentiles"

The Greek word *ethnos* (often translated "Gentiles") simply means "nations." In context:

- When referring to Israel: "nations" of Israel (the scattered tribes)
- When referring to non-Israelites: foreign nations

Paul's mission to the "Gentiles" was a mission to the **nations of Israel** scattered abroad, not to non-Israelites.

> **Romans 11:13**: "For I speak to you Gentiles [*ethnos*: nations], inasmuch as I am the apostle of the Gentiles [nations]."

Paul was the apostle to the nations of scattered Israel in Europe.

---

## Synthesis

The destinations of the apostolic letters form a clear pattern:

1. **All European or Anatolian**: No letters to Africa, Asia, or elsewhere
2. **Greek-speaking regions**: The language of the scattered Israelites
3. **Explicitly addressed to Israel**: James says "twelve tribes," Peter quotes Hosea about Israel
4. **Paul claims kinship**: Calls the Romans his "kinsmen" and "Israelites"

If the apostles wrote to the lost sheep of Israel, and they wrote to Europeans, then Europeans are the lost sheep of Israel.
