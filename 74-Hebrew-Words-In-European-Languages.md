
# Proof 74: Hebrew Words in European Languages

## The Claim

European languages contain Hebrew words, roots, and names that cannot be explained by mere Christian borrowing. These words entered European languages through Israelite migrations, not just through the Bible.

---

## Religious Vocabulary

### Words Through Christianity

Some Hebrew words came through Christian influence:
- Amen, Hallelujah, Sabbath
- Messiah, Satan, Cherub
- Psalm, Rabbi, Pharisee

This is expected — Christianity transmitted these.

### Words Beyond Religion

But other Hebrew words in European languages suggest deeper connection — words that predate or go beyond Christian transmission.

## The Punic Connection

### Phoenician/Hebrew in Western Mediterranean

Punic (Carthaginian) was virtually identical to Hebrew. Punic inscriptions exist in:
- Spain
- Sardinia
- North Africa
- Sicily

Phoenician traders (Israelites) brought Semitic vocabulary westward.

## Linguistic Families

### Indo-European and Semitic

Mainstream linguistics separates:
- Indo-European languages (most European)
- Semitic languages (Hebrew, Arabic)

But some researchers note:
- Unexpected Semitic words in Indo-European languages
- Structural parallels
- Possible ancient contact or common origin

### The Migration Effect

If Israelites migrated to Europe:
- They would bring Hebrew vocabulary
- It would mix with local languages
- Hebrew words would become "European"

1. Celtic vocabulary predates Christianity in those regions

### The Migration Explanation

Israelites migrating to Europe:
- Brought their language
- Mixed with local populations
- Left Hebrew residue in European languages
- Named places after their heritage
