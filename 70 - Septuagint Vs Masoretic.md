
# Proof 70: The Septuagint vs. the Masoretic

## The Claim

The Septuagint (Greek Old Testament) predates the Masoretic (Hebrew) text and was the Bible used by Christ, the apostles, and the early church. Differences between texts may reflect deliberate changes by post-Christian Jews to obscure prophecies.

---

## What Is the Septuagint?

### Background

- Greek translation of Hebrew scriptures
- Translated ~250 BC in Alexandria
- Used by Greek-speaking Jews (diaspora)
- Called "LXX" (70 translators)
- Predates Christ by ~250 years

### Its Importance

The Septuagint:
- Was the Bible of the early church
- Is quoted in the New Testament
- Preserves older readings than Masoretic
- Was Israel's Bible in the diaspora

---

## What Is the Masoretic Text?

### Background

- Hebrew text standardized by Masoretes
- Finalized ~7th-10th century AD
- Based on earlier manuscripts (but which?)
- The basis for most modern OT translations

### The Question

The Masoretic text was finalized by Jews who:
- Rejected Christ
- Were hostile to Christianity
- May have altered texts to counter Christian arguments

---

## New Testament Use of Septuagint

### Quotations

The NT quotes the OT hundreds of times. Many follow the Septuagint rather than the Hebrew:

| Passage | NT Quote | Matches |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Isaiah 7:14 | Matthew 1:23 "virgin" | LXX (*parthenos*) |
| Hebrews 10:5 | "a body hast thou prepared me" | LXX reading |
| Acts 15:17 | "residue of men" | LXX (not Hebrew) |

Christ and the apostles used the Septuagint.

---

## Key Differences

### Isaiah 7:14

**Septuagint:** "Behold, a **virgin** (*parthenos*) shall conceive"
**Masoretic:** "Behold, a **young woman** (*almah*) shall conceive"

The LXX clearly says "virgin"; the MT is ambiguous. Did Jews change this to undermine the virgin birth?

### Chronologies

Genesis 5 and 11 genealogies:
- Septuagint gives longer lifespans
- Masoretic gives shorter
- Affects dating of creation and Flood

### Deuteronomy 32:8

**Septuagint:** "He set the bounds of the peoples according to the number of the **sons of God**"
**Masoretic:** "according to the number of the **children of Israel**"

Dead Sea Scrolls support LXX reading. Did Masoretes change it?

---

## Dead Sea Scrolls Evidence

### Discovered 1947-1956

The Dead Sea Scrolls:
- Date from ~200 BC - 70 AD
- Predate the Masoretic finalization
- Often agree with Septuagint
- Sometimes show Masoretic corruption

### Example: Isaiah

The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaª):
- Shows text similar to Masoretic
- But some variants closer to LXX
- Demonstrates textual diversity

## Why It Matters

### The Apostolic Bible

If the Septuagint was the apostolic Bible:
- The NT was written for LXX readers
- Christians should value LXX readings
- Masoretic changes may be anti-Christian

### Preservation Through Europe

The Septuagint was preserved by:
- Greek-speaking Christians
- European churches
- Copied throughout Christendom

Europeans (Israel) preserved the older text.

---

## Examples of Possible Changes

### Psalm 22:16

**Septuagint/DSS:** "They **pierced** my hands and feet"
**Masoretic:** "Like a **lion**, my hands and feet"

The Masoretic reading makes no grammatical sense. Did Jews change "pierced" to avoid the crucifixion reference?

### Messianic Prophecies

Many Messianic passages show LXX/MT differences:
- Often LXX is clearer about Christ
- MT may have been altered
- The pattern suggests intentionality

---

## The Implications

### Which Text Is Reliable?

For Christians:
- The LXX has strong claims to authority
- It was the apostolic Bible
- The NT often quotes it
- It preserves older readings

### Jews' Rejection

Post-Christian Jews:
- Rejected the LXX (too "Christian")
- Produced new Greek versions (Aquila, etc.)
- Standardized the Masoretic
- Possibly altered Messianic texts

---

## Synthesis

The Septuagint vs. Masoretic question proves:

1. **LXX is older** — Predates Christ
2. **Apostolic use** — NT quotes LXX
3. **Possible corruption** — MT may be altered
4. **European preservation** — Christians kept the LXX
5. **Israel's Bible** — The diaspora used Greek Scripture

The Bible preserved by Europeans (Israel) includes the older Greek tradition.
