
# Proof 29: What "Saint" Really Means

## The Claim

The word "saint" means "holy one" or "set-apart one." In the Old Testament, Israel was the holy, set-apart people. New Testament "saints" are the same people, Israelites sanctified through Christ.

---

## The Hebrew Word

### Qadosh (קָדוֹשׁ)

**Meaning:**
- Holy, sacred
- Set apart, separated
- Consecrated for special purpose

**Old Testament usage for Israel:**

> **Exodus 19:6**: "And ye shall be unto me a kingdom of priests, and an **holy** [*qadosh*] nation."

> **Deuteronomy 7:6**: "For thou art an **holy people** [*am qadosh*] unto the LORD thy God: the LORD thy God hath chosen thee to be a special people unto himself."

> **Deuteronomy 14:2**: "For thou art an **holy people** unto the LORD thy God."

> **Deuteronomy 14:21**: "For thou art an **holy people** unto the LORD thy God."

Israel was uniquely the "holy people", set apart from all nations.

---

## The Greek Word

### Hagios (ἅγιος)

**Meaning:**
- Holy, sacred
- Set apart
- Saint (when used as noun)

**Septuagint usage:**

The Septuagint translates *qadosh* as *hagios*. The Greek "saints" (*hagioi*) are the Hebrew "holy ones" (*qadoshim*).

---

## Saints in the Old Testament

### Daniel's Prophecy

> **Daniel 7:18**: "But the **saints** of the most High shall take the kingdom, and possess the kingdom for ever."

> **Daniel 7:21-22**: "I beheld, and the same horn made war with the **saints**, and prevailed against them; Until the Ancient of days came, and judgment was given to the **saints** of the most High."

> **Daniel 7:27**: "And the kingdom and dominion... shall be given to the people of the **saints** of the most High."

The "saints" in Daniel are Israel, the holy people who will inherit the kingdom.

### Psalm 16:3

> "But to the **saints** that are in the earth, and to the excellent, in whom is all my delight."

---

## Saints in the New Testament

### Applied to Christian Believers

> **Romans 1:7**: "To all that be in Rome, beloved of God, called to be **saints**."

> **1 Corinthians 1:2**: "Unto the church of God which is at Corinth, to them that are sanctified in Christ Jesus, called to be **saints**."

> **Ephesians 1:1**: "Paul, an apostle of Jesus Christ by the will of God, to the **saints** which are at Ephesus."

> **Philippians 1:1**: "To all the **saints** in Christ Jesus which are at Philippi."

> **Colossians 1:2**: "To the **saints** and faithful brethren in Christ which are at Colosse."

The New Testament letters address "saints", the holy ones, in European cities.

---

## The Connection

### Same Word, Same People

| Old Testament | New Testament |
|---------------|---------------|
| *Qadosh* (Hebrew) | *Hagios* (Greek) |
| Israel is holy people | Christians are saints |
| Set apart from nations | Called out (ekklesia) |
| Daniel's saints inherit kingdom | Saints in Christ inherit |

The "saints" of the New Testament are the "holy people" of the Old Testament, Israel.

---

## Set Apart from Whom?

### The Nature of Holiness

"Holy" fundamentally means "separated, set apart."

Israel was set apart from:
- Other nations
- Other peoples
- The world

> **Leviticus 20:24-26**: "I am the LORD your God, which have **separated** you from other people... And ye shall be **holy** unto me: for I the LORD am holy, and have **severed you from other people**, that ye should be mine."

This separation was:
- Ethnic: from other peoples
- Spiritual: for Yahweh's purposes
- Practical: different laws, customs, identity

---

## New Testament Holiness

### Continued Separation

> **1 Peter 2:9**: "But ye are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, an **holy nation**, a peculiar people."

Peter quotes Exodus 19:6 and applies it to his European audience, because they are the holy nation, Israel.

> **2 Corinthians 6:17**: "Wherefore **come out from among them, and be ye separate**, saith the Lord, and touch not the unclean thing; and I will receive you."

Paul quotes Isaiah 52:11, calling for the same separation Israel was commanded.

---

## Not Everyone Is a Saint

### Exclusivity

The term "saint" is not universal:
- Only Israel was "holy people"
- Only those in Christ are "saints"
- Not all humanity is set apart

> **Romans 9:6-8**: "For they are not all Israel, which are of Israel: Neither, because they are the seed of Abraham, are they all children... the children of the promise are counted for the seed."

Even within Israel, not all are counted as the holy seed. The "saints" are the faithful of Israel, those who accept Christ.

---

## Synthesis

"Saint" proves Israelite identity because:

1. **Same word**: *Qadosh/hagios* = holy one
2. **Same people**: Israel was the holy nation
3. **Same concept**: Set apart from other peoples
4. **Applied to Europeans**: New Testament "saints" are in Rome, Corinth, Ephesus
5. **Continuity**: Not a new category, but Israel's identity continued

When Paul writes to "the saints in Rome," he writes to Israelites, the holy people of God.
